# Assessment — The Five Proposed CANDO Topics

**Compiled:** 14 August 2026
**Scope:** data availability (verified against sources, not asserted), difficulty, speed to first result, hidden problems, and what to do if company data never arrives.
**Related:** [Agriculture shortlist](ghana-agriculture-thesis-topics.md) · [Health tech shortlist](health-tech-education-thesis-topics.md) · [Six-candidate comparison](considering.md)

## The headline finding

**All five topics, as written, depend on company data — and the two ranked highest depend on the company's most sensitive data of all.** Invoices, buyer records, payment details and email traffic are simultaneously the most commercially confidential and the most legally protected data a Ghanaian exporter holds. A firm that enthusiastically agrees to "support a student project" in principle very often declines at the point of signing a data-sharing agreement covering exactly this material.

That is the same failure mode that has already cost one thesis. It is not a reason to reject these topics — it is a reason to build each one with a **public-data spine and company data as enrichment**, rather than the reverse.

Three specific findings from verification change the ranking sharply:

**Finding 1 — There is essentially no public BEC data.** The first public Business Email Compromise dataset, BEC-2, contains **279 emails**, generated by a large language model rather than observed in the wild. The one substantial labelled corpus (BEC-Guard: ~7,000 labelled BEC attacks across 1,500 organisations) is proprietary and unavailable, and the literature explicitly identifies this as a persistent barrier to reproducibility in BEC research. Topic 1 is titled *"Cross-Dataset Generalisation"* — but there is almost nothing to generalise across.

**Finding 2 — Public fraud benchmarks are abundant, and a cross-domain benchmark already exists.** IEEE-CIS (590,540 transactions, 393 features), PaySim (synthetic mobile money, derived from real logs from a mobile money service **in an African country**), Elliptic (Bitcoin, 166 features), Sparkov (1.85M transactions), Amazon Fraud, and the published Fraud Dataset Benchmark (FDB). This makes Topic 5 executable on public data alone — but it also means Topic 5 must position itself carefully against work that already exists.

**Finding 3 — and this is the most useful one — export fraud *can* be studied with entirely public data, just not the kind of fraud these topics name.** Trade mis-invoicing — deliberate misreporting of value, quantity or nature of goods, described by the World Customs Organization as the dominant channel of illicit financial flows — is detectable through **mirror statistics**: comparing what Ghana reports exporting with what its partners report importing, at HS-6 level in Comtrade. Global Financial Integrity's methodology is published, the data is free, and it has already been applied to Ghana. This is real, quantifiable, high-value export fraud with **zero gatekeeper dependency**.

## Topic-by-topic

### Topic 1 — Behavioral Cybersecurity for BEC Detection

> *A Behavioral Learning Analytics Framework for Detecting Business Email Compromise in Ghana's Export Supply Chains*

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Public data** | ❌ **Effectively none.** BEC-2 = 279 LLM-generated emails. Adjacent phishing corpora exist but are a different attack class. |
| **Company data needed** | Email traffic, invoices, payment records, buyer details — the most sensitive category a firm holds |
| **Build difficulty** | Medium (the ML is standard) |
| **Time to first result** | **Very slow** — gated entirely on data access, not on modelling |
| **Ethics/legal** | NDA + data-sharing agreement + Data Protection Act 2012 compliance + likely IRB (email content is personal data) |
| **Revised feasibility** | **3/10** |

**The fatal problem is not access — it's labels.** BEC is a *rare event*. A single Ghanaian exporter might have experienced somewhere between zero and a handful of confirmed BEC incidents in its entire history. You cannot train a supervised classifier on single-digit positives, and no amount of company cooperation fixes that. This is a structural property of the problem, not a data-collection shortfall.

The title compounds this by promising **cross-dataset generalisation** — which requires at least two datasets. There is one, and it has 279 synthetic emails.

**What would rescue it.** Reframe from *supervised detection* to *unsupervised anomaly detection on payment-instruction changes* — flagging when a buyer's banking details change, when an invoice deviates from a counterparty's established pattern, when payment routing shifts. This needs no fraud labels because it models normality and flags deviation. Alternatively, pivot the fraud target to trade mis-invoicing (see Topic 5 below), where labels are derivable from public mirror statistics.

**Verdict: do not attempt as written.** The label scarcity is not negotiable.

### Topic 2 — AI Ethics in Export Market Recommendation

> *Algorithmic Fairness and Bias in AI-Driven Export Market Selection*

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Public data** | ✅ **Excellent** — UN Comtrade, GEPA NTE reports, CEPII gravity, WITS tariffs. Enough to build and audit a real recommender. |
| **Company data needed** | **None required.** Company transaction history would enrich but is not load-bearing. |
| **Build difficulty** | Medium |
| **Time to first result** | **Fast** — you can have a working recommender in two weeks |
| **Ethics/legal** | None |
| **Revised feasibility** | **8/10** *(after the reframe below — 5/10 as currently written)* |

**This is the biggest upward revision in the set, and it hinges on fixing a conceptual flaw.** As written, the topic borrows "algorithmic fairness" from a human-subjects context where it depends on a *protected attribute* — race, sex, disability. Countries are not a protected class, and an examiner will ask what exactly is being protected. Left unaddressed, this sinks the thesis at viva.

**The reframe that fixes it — and makes the topic genuinely strong:** the phenomenon being described is not fairness, it is **popularity bias and concentration in recommender systems**, a well-established and rigorously measurable problem. The disadvantaged group is not a country but the **SME and smallholder supplier** whose viable markets are systematically under-surfaced by a recommender trained on historical volume.

Reframed, you build:
1. A market recommender — gravity-based, collaborative filtering, and learning-to-rank variants trained on historical Ghanaian export flows.
2. A **bias audit**: recommendation concentration (Gini/HHI), long-tail coverage, and the gap between what the recommender surfaces and what a gravity model says is *achievable* — which links directly to the export-potential work in the agriculture document.
3. A **feedback-loop simulation** — the genuinely novel component. If exporters follow the recommender, and the recommender retrains on the resulting flows, does Ghana's export base concentrate further over time? This is a real, measurable, and under-studied risk in trade-recommendation systems, and it is the finding a Q1 reviewer would care about.
4. A **de-biasing intervention** with a measured cost: how much recommendation accuracy must be sacrificed to achieve a given gain in market diversification?

**Verdict: strong, once reframed.** The fastest of the five to first result, and the only one that needs nothing from the company.

### Topic 3 — AI Tutor for Export Compliance Training

> *Designing and Evaluating an AI-Powered Tutor for Export Compliance Training*

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Public data** | ⚠️ Compliance *content* is public (HS nomenclature, Incoterms, GEPA/Customs procedures). **Learning data does not exist — you must generate it.** |
| **Company data needed** | Staff participation, which is harder to get than data |
| **Build difficulty** | **High — this is three projects** |
| **Time to first result** | **Slowest of the five by a wide margin** |
| **Ethics/legal** | **Full human-subjects IRB required.** Four to twelve weeks, before any data collection. |
| **Revised feasibility** | **4/10** *(7/10 only if a captive cohort of 60+ learners is already secured)* |

**The problem is that this is three theses wearing one title.** You must (a) build a functioning AI tutor, (b) recruit and retain enough learners to produce analysable data, (c) run a study design capable of supporting a causal claim, and (d) clear ethics before any of it starts. Each is a legitimate master's project on its own.

The binding constraint is **N**. Learning analytics needs enough learners generating enough interaction events to model. A pre/post comparison on 15 participants supports nothing. You would need roughly 60+ learners, ideally with a control condition, and you would need them to actually use the tutor repeatedly rather than once.

**The one condition under which this becomes attractive:** if a cohort already exists and is compelled to participate — a class you can access, a mandatory staff training programme the company is running anyway. If a supervisor can hand you 80 supply chain students as a captive cohort, this jumps to 7/10 and becomes the topic with the clearest "student value" story. **Establish whether that cohort exists before considering this further** — everything depends on it.

**Verdict: highest effort, longest timeline, most dependent on a factor outside your control.** Viable only with a confirmed cohort.

### Topic 4 — Predictive Analytics for Export Disruption and Payment Delay

> *Predicting Export Disruptions and Buyer Payment Delays Using Behavioral and Transactional Features*

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Public data** | ⚠️ Partial — disruption proxies are public (port throughput, shipping indices, weather, RASFF rejections, exchange rates); **payment delay data is not** |
| **Company data needed** | Order and invoice records with payment dates. **Operational, not security-sensitive — far more likely to be shared.** |
| **Build difficulty** | Low-medium (standard supervised learning; survival analysis if done well) |
| **Time to first result** | Fast **once data lands** |
| **Ethics/legal** | Data-sharing agreement; buyer identifiers must be pseudonymised |
| **Revised feasibility** | **7/10** *(with company data) · 4/10 (without)* |

**This is the strongest of the five as originally written, for one reason that the framing undersells: the labels already exist and are abundant.** Every invoice has an issue date and a payment date. Days-late is recorded as a natural by-product of doing business — no rare-event problem, no annotation effort, no fraud-label scarcity. That single property makes it categorically more tractable than Topics 1 and 5.

It is also the company data most likely to actually be released. A firm that will never hand over its email archive will often share pseudonymised order and payment records, because operational data feels commercial rather than confidential.

**A methodological upgrade worth taking:** frame payment delay as **time-to-payment survival analysis** rather than binary late/not-late classification. This handles invoices not yet paid at the observation cut-off (right-censoring) correctly — binary classification either discards them or mislabels them — and yields a far richer output: the probability an invoice remains unpaid at 30, 60, 90 days, per buyer and per destination. That is what a cashflow planner actually needs, and it is a more sophisticated demonstration than a classifier.

**The weak point is "cross-firm validation" in the title.** That requires two or more firms, which doubles the access problem. Either secure a second firm early — a FAGE-mediated introduction is the obvious route — or drop the claim from the title and validate temporally instead (train on earlier periods, test on later), which is a legitimate and defensible substitute.

**Verdict: best risk-adjusted choice of the five if company data is genuinely secured.**

### Topic 5 — Cross-Domain Generalisation of Fraud Detection Models

> *A Comparison of Real, Public, and Synthetic Data in Agricultural Export Supply Chains*

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Public data** | ✅ **Abundant** — IEEE-CIS, PaySim, Elliptic, Sparkov, Amazon Fraud, plus the published Fraud Dataset Benchmark |
| **Company data needed** | Desirable for the "real" arm; **the study still runs without it** |
| **Build difficulty** | Medium |
| **Time to first result** | **Fast** — datasets download today |
| **Ethics/legal** | None for the public-data arms |
| **Revised feasibility** | **7/10** *(and it is the only one of the five that cannot be blocked)* |

**This is the most robust of the five, because it degrades gracefully.** If company data arrives, you have a three-arm comparison. If it never arrives, you still have a complete two-arm study on public and synthetic data. No other topic in the set survives its data source failing.

Two things must be handled honestly:

**First, position against what already exists.** A published Fraud Dataset Benchmark already provides cross-dataset fraud comparison. Your contribution cannot be "we compared fraud datasets." It has to be the **agricultural-export-specific angle** — and that angle is where the topic currently has nothing, because none of the public datasets are trade datasets. This is the gap you must close, and the next paragraph is how.

**Second, recent work suggests your headline finding may already be pointing in one direction.** A 2026 benchmark found that synthetic tabular generators **fail to preserve behavioural fraud patterns** — specifically the temporal, velocity and multi-account signals that fraud detection depends on. This does not kill the topic; it sharpens it. Replicating and extending that finding in a trade context, rather than discovering it fresh, is a legitimate and stronger contribution. Just do not design the thesis assuming synthetic data will work.

**The reframe that gives this topic its Ghana and its agriculture:** shift the fraud target from card/transaction fraud to **trade mis-invoicing**, detected through mirror statistics — comparing Ghana's reported exports against partners' reported imports at HS-6 level in Comtrade. The methodology is published, the data is free, the anomalies are quantifiable, and it has already been applied to Ghana. That gives you a genuinely agricultural-export fraud dataset built entirely from public sources, which you can then compare against the public financial-fraud benchmarks and synthetic generators. **That version of Topic 5 is the strongest single idea in this entire set of five.**

**Verdict: the most resilient topic, and outstanding once the fraud target is shifted to mis-invoicing.**

## Revised ranking

The original ranking was built on company value. This one is built on whether the thesis can actually be completed.

| New rank | Topic | Original rank | Data risk | Difficulty | Speed | Feasibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **T5 — Cross-domain fraud generalisation** *(retargeted to trade mis-invoicing)* | 5 | **None** | Medium | Fast | **7→9/10** |
| **2** | **T2 — Export market recommender bias** *(reframed from "fairness")* | 2 | **None** | Medium | **Fastest** | **8/10** |
| **3** | **T4 — Payment delay & disruption prediction** | 4 | Moderate | Low-med | Fast once data lands | **7/10** |
| 4 | T3 — AI tutor for export compliance | 3 | High | **Highest** | **Slowest** | 4/10 *(7 with a secured cohort)* |
| 5 | T1 — BEC detection | **1** | **Severe** | Medium | Very slow | **3/10** |

**The topic ranked first by company value is ranked last here.** That is not a disagreement about its value — BEC fraud is a real and expensive problem for Ghanaian exporters. It is a statement about whether a master's thesis can be built on it. Rare events with single-digit positives and no public corpus do not support a supervised model, and no amount of goodwill from the company changes that.

## The recommendation

> ### Topic 5, retargeted: **cross-domain fraud detection with trade mis-invoicing as the agricultural-export arm**

Working title: *Detecting Trade Mis-Invoicing in Ghana's Agricultural Exports: Cross-Domain Model Generalisation Across Real Trade, Public Financial, and Synthetic Fraud Data*

**Why this one:**

1. **It cannot be blocked.** Comtrade and every public fraud benchmark download today. Company data becomes a bonus validation chapter rather than a dependency.
2. **It has real labels.** Mirror-statistic value gaps give quantifiable anomaly signals at HS-6 product–partner level — no annotation, no rare-event famine, no waiting for an incident log.
3. **It is genuinely about agricultural export supply chains**, which the original Topic 5 was not. The Ghana content is structural rather than decorative.
4. **It keeps the CANDO fit intact** — AI + Data + Cybersecurity, with fraud and illicit financial flows squarely inside the cybersecurity and financial-crime space.
5. **It degrades gracefully.** Every component has a fallback. Nothing in it has a single point of failure.
6. **The company value survives.** A model that flags anomalous trade declarations is directly useful to an exporter for counterparty screening and to GRA Customs for audit targeting — arguably broader value than a BEC filter serving one firm.

**Run this check first, in one day:** compute Ghana's export mirror gaps for the main horticultural HS codes and confirm the anomaly signal is present and non-trivial. If the gaps are large and structured, proceed. If they are flat, fall back to **Topic 2**, which needs nothing but Comtrade and is the fastest of the five to a working result.

## Questions to resolve with the company before committing

These determine which topics are live and should be answered before a proposal is written, not after.

1. **Is the data-sharing agreement signed, or is support currently verbal?** Enthusiasm at a meeting and a signature on a DSA covering invoices and payment records are very different things. Topics 1, 3 and 4 all rest on the second.
2. **How many confirmed BEC or invoice-fraud incidents does the company actually have on record?** If the answer is under 20, Topic 1 is not viable at any level of cooperation — ask this early and directly.
3. **Does a captive cohort of 60+ learners exist for the AI tutor?** If not, Topic 3 has no data source and should be dropped from consideration.
4. **Would the company share pseudonymised order and payment records specifically** — separable from email and buyer-identity data? This is the single question that decides Topic 4, and it is worth asking as its own question rather than bundled with a general data request.
5. **Is a second firm available for cross-firm validation?** FAGE membership is the natural route. If not, remove "cross-firm" from Topic 4's title and validate temporally instead.
6. **Who owns the resulting model?** Worth settling in writing at the start. It affects what you can publish, and Q1 publication is stated as a goal for all five.

---

## A note on the Q1 publication targets

The journals named are correctly matched to the topics, and several are genuinely Q1. But a master's thesis rarely reaches Q1 without substantial post-submission extension, and — importantly — **the constraint is almost always the data, not the method.** *Computers & Security* will not publish a BEC detection paper trained on 279 synthetic emails regardless of how good the modelling is. *Expert Systems with Applications* and *Decision Support Systems* will consider a cross-domain generalisation study with a novel, defensible dataset — which is exactly what the mis-invoicing retarget provides.

Treat Q1 as a realistic outcome for Topics 5 and 2, an aspiration for Topic 4, and a non-starter for Topic 1 as written.
